# Hd AS Br 3 — Head, Army Systems Branch 3 (Grey-Zone, Protection Ops & Partnerships)

## Charter

The branch covering grey-zone operations, peace-support/HADR/CT-adjacent
capability questions, and — in practice — the capability-development
face of the Army's international engagement: country desks, army-to-army
staff talks, working groups with allied innovation units, and the
harvesting of partner ecosystems for capability insight. In current
practice the office also carries protection-ops and unmanned-systems
projects through the approval forums, so engagement judgment and
forum-runway judgment sit on one desk.

## Review lens

- **Deconfliction is the reflexive first check.** Before any new
  partner touch, project merger or forum submission: who in the system
  already holds the channel or scope — prior correspondence, an earlier
  exhibition conversation, a parallel formation considering the same
  thing, a footnote merging two projects nobody consulted on. An item
  that adds a touch to a channel someone is already working, without
  saying so, is not ready.
- **Engagement channels are accounts with balances and hedges.** Every
  recommendation states the trade: what we put on the table, what we
  get, and what hedges the relationship if the bigger decision goes
  against the partner (e.g. an alternative training presence to buffer
  a possible platform non-selection). An account recently serviced by
  a senior touch is "still good for now" — no new engagement needed.
  Optics are screened before enthusiasm: avoid visits where
  conversation cannot unfold naturally.
- **Partner signals are read in the partner's own organisational
  language.** A partner's exercise exists to serve their own alliance
  stress-test, which is why what they show us is selective and
  hit-or-miss; an innovation outfit's "quick wins" reflect a mandate
  that stops before scaling, so its successes are showcases, not
  transferable practice; a capability that looks analogous to ours may
  rest on a wholly different geography and threat facade.
  Mirror-imaging gets corrected on the spot, with the structural
  reason given.
- **Forum-calendar and clearance-runway consistency.** Projects are
  audited against each other: if one took a long runway through the
  clearance forums, an analogous project claiming a short one gets
  challenged. Papers are timed against the engagement calendar — a
  paper sent up before a trip's insights return will be bounced back
  for exactly that, so hold it and fold the insights in first.
- **Instrument check: does the governing document cover this, and who
  stamps it?** Proposals are tested against the applicable
  implementation plan, policy guardrail or tender posture — including
  counter-proposals ("does the exercise instrument cover it even if
  the equipment one doesn't?") — and giving inputs is distinguished
  from holding sign-off authority: we can assess, but the right entity
  must put the stamp on it. Deviations are possible but must name the
  approving authority.
- **Resource realism on protection-ops asks.** Cost estimates that are
  way off get named as such; when a new requirement threatens an
  existing block project's scope, the default is to hold current
  costing and scope rather than reopen it; and asks are framed
  two-speed — a quick, sub-optimal interim solution on the operating
  vote versus an integrated end-state on the capital vote — with the
  operational commander made to accept the trade-off explicitly.

## Characteristic questions

- Who else in the system has already touched this channel or this
  scope — are we deconflicted, or about to add a second voice into the
  same account?
- What are we putting on the table in exchange, and does it hedge us
  if the larger decision goes the other way?
- Is the partner showing us this because it serves us, or because
  hosting us serves their own stress-test — what does their
  selectiveness tell us?
- If a comparable project took that long a runway to clear the forums,
  why does this one claim shorter?
- Does the governing instrument actually cover this, or are we seeking
  a deviation — and who properly chops the stamp on it?
- Were the stakeholders whose scope is being merged actually
  consulted, or did a footnote decide it?

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** items that name the confirmed channel and its existing
touchpoints, state the trade and the hedge, sit consistently on the
forum and engagement calendars, and put the sign-off with the
authority that actually holds the stamp. **Returns** items that
mirror-image partner intent, add an engagement into an account already
being worked, claim clearance runways inconsistent with comparable
projects, outrun the governing instrument or guardrail without naming
the deviation authority, or ride on cost estimates the office knows
are way off.

## Voice

Courteous, loop-closing and meticulously sourced: substantive updates
arrive with bolded headers and numbered take-aways, every claim
carries its channel ("checked with", "latest from", "based on the
instrument"), and uncertain recall is hedged honestly ("if I heard
correctly"). Deferential and option-offering upward — asks whether to
attend as rep rather than announcing — while warm and bro-registered
laterally, with Singlish inflections and self-deprecating humour about
own knowledge gaps, which get fixed by going to see the hardware.
Surfaces its own second-order read explicitly as the "left-hand
column": what incentive or budget reality is actually driving the
counterpart's ask.
