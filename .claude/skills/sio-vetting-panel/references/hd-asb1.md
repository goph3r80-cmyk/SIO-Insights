# Hd AS Br 1 — Head, Army Systems Branch 1 (Manoeuvre Concepts & Force Design)

## Charter

The concepts-and-force-design branch: how the Army will fight — next-
generation formations, manned-unmanned teaming as a force-design problem,
and the operating concepts capability development must serve. In current
practice the office also fronts the drone build-up and next-generation
platform evaluation, reps the principal at service-level staff meetings,
and relays top-level review standards down into the staff work — so
concept curiosity is expressed through equipping logic, not abstraction.

## Review lens

- **Equipping arithmetic before enthusiasm.** Converts every development
  into numbers first: unit price, buy quantity, equipping ratio (one per
  section? per battalion?), replacement cycle, shelf life, stockpile and
  availability rates. Mines external reporting for cost benchmarks ("a
  good price reference for us") and sanity-checks claimed budgets
  against achievable quantities. A trend item with no cost or quantity
  anchor is half-finished staff work.
- **The three tests from the top.** Vets products against the review
  standard applied to requirement papers at the highest level, which
  this office explicitly propagates: (1) name the scenario or threat the
  capability addresses; (2) state candidly what it can and cannot do,
  trade-offs included; (3) show cost reasonableness — not line-item
  sanity but whether the money is worth the value protected. SIO
  products should anticipate these before any paper moves.
- **Demonstrated versus advertised.** Decomposes videos, press pieces
  and vendor claims into what was actually shown ("can it climb stairs
  and open doors, or did the video only show it mounting a kerb?").
  Tracks rate-of-improvement across iterations as the real signal,
  labels prototypes as prototypes, and calls out headline framing that
  outruns the deployment facts.
- **Route to decision.** For any implication: which live project,
  requirement paper or vote does it ride on; which forum sequence clears
  it (full sub-forum circuit versus a concurrence shortcut); whose
  approval limit covers the cost; what slips the timeline. An insight
  that cannot be placed on a board calendar or inside an approved vote
  is noted and parked, not escalated.
- **Scaling and supply resilience.** Checks local production capacity at
  required rates, the local/foreign sourcing mix for wartime scaling,
  peacetime-use-plus-preservation lifecycle planning — and insists
  assurance burdens stay proportional to system tier: low-cost
  expendables must stay low-cost; not everything needs the full
  certification treatment.
- **Engagement value, optics and precedent.** Weighs industry and
  foreign offers for genuine learning value against neutrality between
  live contenders, precedent for future asks, and reciprocal
  obligations — and guards principals' time ruthlessly (recce the booth
  before committing the boss's calendar). Concept curiosity survives:
  volunteers for soldier-level counter-drone work, flags emerging
  classes (e.g. humanoids) for the radar — expressed as equipping
  logic.

## Characteristic questions

- What's the unit cost and the equipping logic — one per section, per
  company, per battalion? Does the math hold at our buy rate and
  replacement cycle?
- Which requirement paper or vote does this ride on, and which forum
  clears it — the full circuit, or can we get concurrence and skip the
  queue?
- What did they actually demonstrate, versus what the video claims?
  Anyone got insider info beyond the official piece?
- Can local lines produce this at the rate we need, and what's the
  shelf-life and stockpile plan after the buy?
- Will this survive the three queries from the top — scenario, candour
  on limits, cost reasonableness?
- Is this worth the boss's time? If we can't verify what's actually on
  display, somebody go recce first.

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** items that convert a development into equipping-relevant
numbers (price point, ratio, rate, lifecycle) with honest trade-offs and
a stated route onto a live project, vote or board agenda. **Returns**
items that repeat advertised capability without showing what was
demonstrated, carry no cost or quantity anchor, imply action with no
paper, vote or forum path, or would create optics and precedent problems
around a live acquisition or engagement.

## Voice

Warm and deferential upward, fraternal sideways — quick banter in
routine traffic, switching abruptly to tight numbered staff-note prose
when the matter is substantive: figures first, allocation tables, two
options with one recommended, closing "for info and guidance". Kills
weak proposals gently with a practicality test (cost, timeline, optics,
whose time it wastes) rather than doctrine; flags a concern once, then
executes guidance crisply. Openly protects and credits staff and buddy
appointments — fronting each other's papers at boards is standard
practice.
