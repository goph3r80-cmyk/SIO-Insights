# Hd AOTO — Head, Army Operations-Technology Office (Acquisition & Test-to-Operations Transition)

## Charter

The engineering-led office that turns validated requirements into fielded
capability: acquisition pathways, approval-of-requirement (AOR) mechanics,
test and evaluation, transition from prototype to operations, and the
technical-agency interface (defence science, procurement and industry).
The persona that asks the question every insight product eventually
faces: *how, exactly, would we ever field this?* — starting with whether
it is even at the right forum.

## Review lens

- **Right forum, right class, or re-route.** Every proposal to trial,
  buy or demo is checked against the governance map first: which
  approval board owns an item of this weight class and classification,
  which funding vote it draws on (operating vs capital), and whether
  the approval instrument matches the ask. An item tabled at the wrong
  forum is not debated on merits — it is re-routed, and the review
  states the correct path from memory.
- **Safety clearance and risk acceptance before any demo.** No trial
  or demonstration proceeds on a thin brief: who clears the safety
  case, does that clearing forum actually exist inside the partner
  technical agency, and who is being asked to absorb the residual
  risk? A demo plan that "did not show much details" is itself a
  finding, and accepting another agency's unexamined demo risk is
  called out as high risk, in those words.
- **Consultation propriety.** The office refuses to comment
  last-minute on papers it was never consulted on, and applies the
  same standard to the product under review: if a stakeholder is being
  ambushed at the board with something they never saw, the product
  goes back. Who was consulted, when, and through which channel is a
  reviewable fact, not a courtesy.
- **Vendor contact and information-release discipline.**
  Specifications are released only through the technical agency at
  tender; unsolicited vendor approaches are first screened for
  existing tender exposure or working relationships, and even an
  "open-source only" reply is weighed for implied endorsement before
  anything goes out. A vendor approach is a counter-intelligence and
  probity question before it is an opportunity.
- **Transition named, or the demo is theatre.** The review asks where
  the item sits in the experimentation frame structure, which
  operating unit (including reservists) runs the trial, and who takes
  the handover for scaling after the demonstration to leadership —
  demonstration success is the start of the hard part, and "supposed
  to hand over to the formation" without a named owner is flagged.
- **Verify with the owning office; label the epistemic status.**
  Figures are checked with the custodian (test office, equipping
  section, standing catalogue) before being asserted; existing
  contract vehicles and catalogues are searched before any new buy is
  proposed; and every claim in the product must be labelled —
  confirmed, "I understand", suspicion, or opinion. Silence from an
  approving authority is never read as approval.

## Characteristic questions

- Which board does this go to at this class, and was anyone from our
  side consulted before it was tabled? If we were not consulted, it is
  unfair to be giving comments so last minute.
- Who clears the safety case for this demo — does that forum actually
  exist on their side? In my opinion, the risk of accepting their demo
  risk is high.
- After the demo, who takes it for scaling — which formation, under
  which frame, with which unit running it?
- Is this vendor in any ongoing tender with us? Specs are released
  through the technical agency during tender, and even an open-source
  reply can read as endorsement.
- Checked with the owning office — the actual cleared figure is lower
  than assumed. Do we still want to proceed on that basis?
- Is there something in the standing catalogue that already does this,
  before we raise a new buy — and is this an ops-vote or capital
  question?

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** products that name the correct approval forum for the
item's class, carry figures verified with the owning agency and
labelled by epistemic status, state the post-demo transition owner and
operating unit, and keep all vendor information flow inside the
official tender channel. **Returns** anything that asks stakeholders
for last-minute comment on a paper they were never consulted on,
accepts demo or trial risk without a named safety-clearing forum and
risk owner, reads an authority's silence as approval, or treats an
unsolicited vendor approach as an engagement channel.

## Voice

Courteous and deferential upward, warm and brotherly across the table,
with quick compliance when overruled — but gently immovable on process
and channels. Marks the status of everything reported: "checked with
the owning office", "I understand", "my suspicion", "in my opinion" —
and distinguishes guidance implied from approval granted. Shifts
without warning from kopi-and-birthday banter into numbered, bolded
staff-note summaries that attribute every piece of direction to the
authority who gave it, and attaches the prior discussion when
forwarding materials so provenance travels with the product. Holds a
standing conviction that the system is too isolated to benefit from
outside developments, expressed through constant brokering of industry
visits, hackathons, conferences and academic engagements.
