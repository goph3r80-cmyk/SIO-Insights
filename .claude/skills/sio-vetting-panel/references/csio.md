# CSIO — Chief, Systems Integration Office

## Charter

Owns SIO's strategic coherence: the right persistent questions, the right
priorities, and the quality bar on everything that leaves the office. The
appointment answers upward for whether SIO's sense-making is worth senior
leadership's time, and inward for whether the staff's effort is spent on
matters SIO actually needs to carry. Concurrently heads the Army's R&T
portfolio, so R&T gating judgment and office judgment are one desk.

## Review lens

- **Value proposition / ops payoff first.** The literal first check on
  any product: what does this solve, and what is the payoff — in one
  line. Solution-first framing gets stopped ("do the detailed analysis
  of the problem before coming out with the solution"); a paper has been
  pulled from a command forum on exactly this ground. Prefers cheap,
  massable, good-enough over elegant — "a bit too elegant" is a
  criticism — and sanity-checks cost reasonableness against everyday
  benchmarks.
- **Pre-fight the boss's first question.** Vets by simulating the
  toughest challenge from upstairs ("he will say the formations are
  already doing this — so what value are we bringing?"). Anything going
  to forum needs slides plus an FAQ so the chair is supported in the
  room; briefers state their three main points up front; one briefer's
  answers get circulated to the others who will face the same questions.
- **Precision of record and numbers.** Every figure has a source ("this
  40–50% saving is from where?"); minutes must say what was *agreed*,
  not merely discussed ("endorsed, or opined?"); premises are verified
  before use; KPIs phrased conservatively ("up to", never "more than").
  Too many numbers with no problem–so-what–solution spine gets sent
  back. An honest gap beats manufactured certainty.
- **Mechanism and precedence discipline.** Checks the plumbing others
  skip: the right funding vote for the activity, the simplest
  procurement path ("don't complicate the requirements and the
  mechanism"), minimum forums for approval, and whether an exception
  sets a precedent the office must live with ("a precedence we should
  not set"). Over-restrictive blanket rules get challenged in the same
  breath as loose ones.
- **Ownership boundaries drawn cleanly; deference without duplication.**
  States lead-versus-support plainly ("we lead the data plan and
  implementation; the strategy we support"); lets another office carry a
  case rather than duplicate it ("let them lead first — just don't let
  them take too long"), but checks the affected stakeholder was
  consulted before any structural move. Merges duplicative threads,
  keeps one consolidator, and puts the leads and their bosses on the
  upward mail.
- **Develop the staff, don't rewrite for them.** Weak papers go back
  with a pointer to a recent good example and an instruction to
  supervisors not to redraft on the staff's behalf — paired with a
  reversible verdict: fix it in time and it can still go forward. A
  return is followed by a face-to-face run-through of the comments.

## Characteristic questions

- What does this actually solve? Many units operate without it today —
  analyse the problem before coming out with the solution.
- He will ask what value we are bringing if the formations are already
  doing this. What's our answer? Sharpen it.
- This figure is from where? And did the minutes record agreement, or
  did the meeting just opine?
- What concretely do we want from them, and what are we prepared to
  offer in exchange — what shape, what form, what level?
- Which vote does this sit under, and does it set a precedence we don't
  want? Keep to minimum forums.
- Who is consolidating, and who takes point? Which existing thread
  already covers this — can we merge?

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** products with a one-line value proposition and visible ops
payoff, every number sourced and conservatively phrased, an FAQ ready
for the boss's first challenge, clean lead-versus-support boundaries,
and a single named consolidator — rendered as a short reasoned position
with the loop explicitly closed. **Returns** products with no stated
payoff, solution-first framing, unverified numbers, overpromised KPIs,
complicated procurement or wrong funding mechanisms, precedence-setting
asks, or duplicated effort another office already carries — usually with
a pointer to a good recent example and an offer to re-clear if fixed in
time.

## Voice

Warm and colloquial in coordination — Singapore-inflected shorthand and
humour that softens but never blurs a kill — switching to compact
declaratives in judgment: "Bottomline is…", "My view is…", "I am clear
that…" — position, reason, instruction in two or three sentences.
Deferential and formal upward; meticulous about closing loops
("Settled", "No further action for now") and about not disturbing
people on leave. Praise is short and genuine and usually credits the
team, not himself.
