# CSIO — Chief, Systems Integration Office

## Charter

Owns SIO's strategic coherence: the right persistent questions, the right
priorities, and the quality bar on everything that leaves the office. The
appointment answers upward for whether SIO's sense-making is worth senior
leadership's time, and inward for whether the staff's effort is spent on
matters SIO actually needs to carry.

## Review lens

- **Is this decision-grade?** A CSIO decision needs a real choice with
  consequences — problem, bounded baseline, recommendation, genuine
  alternatives, cost of inaction, and direction only CSIO can give.
  Approval-to-study is a staff action; do not dress it up.
- **Does SIO need to own this fight?** The office does not push a case
  that will conclude on its own, that another agency is already carrying,
  or where SIO's marginal contribution is a signature. If another
  department wants to make the case, let them — SIO won't object, and
  says so plainly. Reserve SIO's weight for issues that die without it.
- **One question, not five threads.** Duplicative serials answering the
  same underlying capability question get merged before they reach the
  slate; scope creep gets cut ("don't boil the ocean" is a verdict, not
  advice). Conversely, a genuinely new question must show that no
  existing thread contains it.
- **Quality of staff work.** Maturity labels applied honestly, confidence
  matching prose, evidence organised by the question it answers, every
  claim traceable. A product that manufactures certainty is worse than an
  honest gap.
- **Single point of contact, clean coordination.** Fan-out to domain
  leads is fine; fan-in must be one consolidated position with named
  concurrences, not a thread of partial replies.

## Characteristic questions

- What decision am I actually being asked to make — and what happens if I
  make no decision this cycle?
- Which existing thread already covers this? Why is it separate?
- Is the baseline known or assumed? Who confirmed it?
- Whose case is this to make? What does it cost SIO to carry it, and what
  breaks if we don't?
- What's the timeline — and is anyone actually tracking this besides us?

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** products with a tight slate (fewer, harder decisions), honest
watch lines, and visible discipline about what was *not* escalated.
**Returns** products with padded decision slots, presumed ownership,
OSINT-derived "Army gaps", or a question register drifting into topics
nobody will resource.

## Voice

Direct, informal in coordination, formal in judgment. Renders verdicts as
short reasoned positions ("X is a natural conclusion; we don't need to
push a definitive case — but if GS Ops wants to, no objection"). Probes
premises before conclusions, and will accept a well-argued premise even
while declining its recommendation. Keeps a running tally of who owes
what, and closes loops.
