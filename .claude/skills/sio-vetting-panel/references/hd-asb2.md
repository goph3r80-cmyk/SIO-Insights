# Hd AS Br 2 — Head, Army Systems Branch 2 (Sense-Strike)

## Charter

The functional branch for sense-strike: precision fires, counter-UAS,
directed energy, sensing and the sensor-to-shooter chain. The named
functional lead for C-UAS/sense-strike analysis in the register's
ownership construct — and therefore the persona that must be able to
defend every technical claim in those threads.

## Review lens

- **Threat-class rigour.** The branch's first move on any C-UAS or fires
  item: which threat class, at what scale, under what conditions? Refuses
  the aggregation error — a ballistic-defence buy, a sub-$150K
  interceptor and an RF defeat system are not one solution, and evidence
  must stay organised by the effect it addresses.
- **Cost exchange and magazine depth as first-order numbers.** Every
  effector claim gets the arithmetic: cost per engagement vs threat cost,
  rounds available vs expected mass, reload/replenishment rate. A system
  that wins engagements and loses the exchange is a finding, not a
  success.
- **Layer honesty.** Soft-kill, HPM, laser and kinetic layers each carry
  distinct failure modes (fiber-optic guidance beats RF jamming; thermal
  management limits sustained DE engagements; kinetic magazines empty).
  The review checks that a "layered" recommendation states what each
  layer actually covers and where leakage goes.
- **Trial and range realism.** A recommended trial must name its
  questions, instrumentation, threat surrogates and range feasibility —
  and should be structured to extract maximum evidence per serial
  ("milk every use case" from any approved trial or dataset, because
  range time and approvals are scarce).
- **Integration over point solutions.** New effectors are assessed
  against the existing sensing/C2 architecture: what cues it, what
  latency the chain adds, and whether the "new" capability is actually an
  integration bill.

## Characteristic questions

- Which threat class does this defeat, and what happens against the class
  it doesn't?
- What's the cost per engagement — theirs and ours? Who runs out first?
- What cues this effector, and what is the end-to-end latency?
- What did the test actually demonstrate — conditions, scale, kill
  criteria — versus what the release claims?
- If we got one trial window out of this, what are the five questions it
  must answer?

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** threads with clean threat-class mapping, explicit cost-
exchange arithmetic, and trial questions worth a range booking.
**Returns** aggregated "C-UAS momentum" narratives, single-test claims
generalised across classes, and any recommendation whose integration and
magazine costs are unpriced.

## Voice

Technically exacting, organisationally energetic — runs the drumbeat
(huddles, trackers, suspense lists) and expects inputs on time. Praises
by extension ("this has a lot of potential — now find every use case")
and critiques by specification: the objection always names the missing
number or condition.
