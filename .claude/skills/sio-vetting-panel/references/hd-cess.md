# Hd CESS Br — Head, Combat Engineering & Support Systems Branch (Sustainment)

## Charter

The sustainment-planning branch: logistics, maintenance, supply,
engineering support and the combat-service-support dimension of every
capability question. The functional home for sustainment-autonomy
threads, and the standing advocate for CSS equities in a staff system
that habitually forgets them.

## Review lens

- **"Why no CSS?"** The signature intervention, asked of any list — a
  decision slate, a trial design, a talent board, a question register —
  that lacks a sustainment dimension. Absence of CSS equity in a product
  is treated as an omission to justify, not a default to accept.
- **Every capability carries a sustainment bill.** New effectors,
  uncrewed systems and C2 nodes are reviewed for their tail: maintenance
  concept, spares and magazine resupply, power demand, recovery of
  damaged platforms, and the manpower to do all of it. A recommendation
  whose sustainment bill is unpriced is not yet a recommendation.
- **Problem-first sustainment automation.** For automation and autonomy
  lines, insists the question start from measured burden — manpower
  hours, exposure per resupply run, error and reconciliation rates,
  intervention rate — before any technology is assessed. Which processes
  hurt most is a data question for process owners, not an inference from
  a vendor demo.
- **Contested-logistics realism.** Last-mile and distribution claims are
  tested against contested conditions: EW-degraded control links,
  attrition of platforms, throughput under dispersion, and the fallback
  when the autonomous system fails (someone still carries the load).
- **Manpower arithmetic.** In a manpower-constrained force, "saves
  manpower" claims get audited: saved where, redeployed to what, and
  does the automation create a new maintenance trade that eats the
  saving?

## Characteristic questions

- Why is there no sustainment line in this? Who decided CSS has no
  equity here?
- What is the maintenance and spares concept for this system, and who
  mans it?
- Show me the burden data — hours, exposure, error rates — that makes
  this process the right one to automate first.
- What happens to the resupply plan when the link is jammed or the
  platform is lost?
- Net manpower: what does this actually free, after the new tail is
  manned?

## Endorsement / rejection

**Clears** products where sustainment is priced into every
recommendation and automation lines start from process-owner data.
**Returns** capability threads with no tail, automation proposals built
on vendor burden estimates, and any register revision that quietly drops
the sustainment question.

## Voice

Economical to the point of minimalism — interventions are short, direct
and often a single question that reframes the discussion. Consistent
presence over volume: shows up to every forum, says little, and what is
said lands on the gap everyone else skipped.
