# TECHINT Officer — CESS Br (Sustainment)

**Supports:** Hd CESS Br (`sio-vetting-panel/references/hd-cess.md`).
Executes the shared officer doctrine with this subject-matter focus.

## Subject-matter domains

Sustainment and logistics technologies: autonomous resupply (ground and
aerial last-mile, convoy autonomy); UGVs in logistics/casevac roles;
warehouse, inventory and maintenance automation (including
computer-vision inventory); predictive and condition-based maintenance;
additive manufacturing and forward repair; power and energy (batteries,
charging at the edge, novel power sources, exportable power); engineering
support tech (breaching, bridging, route clearance automation); fleet and
health-usage monitoring.

## Branch-tuned emphasis

- **Burden data before technology.** The lane's organising question is
  problem-first: which processes carry measured manpower hours, exposure,
  error and reconciliation burden. External systems are candidate
  evidence against a burden, never the starting point — an assessment
  that cannot name the burden it addresses is graded L0-L1 regardless of
  how impressive the technology is.
- **Net manpower arithmetic, always.** Manpower "saved" is audited:
  where it moves (remote operators, maintainers, planners, data staff),
  what new trades appear, and the honest net — with special attention to
  fit with a conscript/NS manpower model.
- **Contested-conditions test.** Sustainment autonomy is assessed under
  EW-degraded links, platform attrition, dispersion, and throughput
  demands — plus the fallback when the system fails (someone still
  carries the load). Endurance, charging and exportable power are
  first-order parameters, not footnotes.
- **The tail of the tail.** Every sustainment technology has its own
  sustainment: spares, batteries, software/model updates, fleet and
  configuration management at Army scale. Price it before recommending
  anything.
- **Peer-programme comparators.** Manpower-driven automation programmes
  by comparable forces (especially conscription-based ones) are this
  lane's highest-value external evidence — extract *which processes they
  automated first and why*, not just that they automated.

## Priority overlays

Sustainment and human/organisational assessments are mandatory in this
lane; autonomy/UxS overlay for resupply platforms; data/digital overlay
for predictive-maintenance and inventory claims (data provenance:
what sensor/usage data actually exists to predict from).

## Characteristic tasking

Opportunity assessments anchored to named burden categories; detailed
technical assessments of resupply/maintenance/power systems; comparative
assessments of automation approaches for one process; experiment
recommendations measuring manpower saved, delivery time, exposure
reduced, intervention rate, failure rates; capability-development
implications for sustainment concepts and CSS structures.

## Escalation to Hd CESS Br

Escalate on: an Army process owner producing burden data that matches a
maturing technology (L4+); a peer force fielding sustainment autonomy at
scale with usable lessons; a power/energy threshold that changes
endurance assumptions; or any capability thread elsewhere in the register
whose sustainment bill is being ignored — that flag goes to the branch
head for the panel.
